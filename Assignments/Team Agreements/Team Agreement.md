These are the terms of group conduct and cooperation that we agree on as a team.

## Participation
**We agree to:**

- Every member writes code. Role titles describe who owns coordination and documentation, not who is exempt from implementation.
- Own our assigned modules end to end: implementation, tests, and the technical documentation for that module. If your module is broken, it is yours to fix or yours to escalate.
- Take on a fair share of the work we like least. Extraction edge cases and evaluation plumbing get distributed, not quietly inherited by whoever complains last.
- Say so early when a task turns out to be bigger than scoped. Reporting a problem in week one is participation; going quiet until the deadline is not.
- Keep our Jira tasks current, because task status is how the other two know what is safe to build on.

## Communication
**We agree to:**

- Use our Discord server as the primary channel, with separate channels for standup, dev, research links, and off-topic. Anything decided elsewhere gets posted there.
- Reply within 24 hours on weekdays and 48 hours on weekends. An emoji reaction counts as acknowledgment; it means "seen," not "agreed."
- Announce in advance when we will be unavailable for more than a couple of days for work, exams, or travel, so the others can plan around it instead of waiting on us.
- Write decisions down in Discord or Jira. A decision settled verbally and never recorded is a decision we will relitigate in three weeks.
- Ask for help before losing a full day to a problem. Being stuck is normal; hiding it costs the team time it cannot get back.

## Meetings
**We agree to:**

- Hold one 30-minute standup per week outside of class, Sunday evening, so the week starts with a shared plan.
- Have Sam post the agenda ahead of the meeting and notes afterward, so the record does not depend on anyone's memory.
- Come prepared to answer three things: what we finished, what we are doing next, and what is blocking us.
- Post a written update in the standup channel beforehand when we cannot attend. Missing a meeting is fine; disappearing from one is not.
- Keep standup to status and blockers. Design arguments get a separate call so they do not consume the whole meeting.

## Conduct
**We agree to:**

- Critique the work, never the person. "This breaks when the policy is behind a JS redirect" is feedback; "you did not think this through" is not.
- Review each other's code honestly. Approving without reading is a disservice to the person who wrote it and to the grade we share.
- Assume good intent when a message reads badly. Text has no tone, and everyone here is busy.
- Give credit specifically and publicly when someone unblocks us or catches something.
- Treat "I do not know" and "I was wrong" as normal engineering statements rather than admissions, since pretending otherwise is how bugs survive to the demo.

## Conflict
**We agree to:**

- Raise disagreements directly with the person involved first, in the channel or on a call, not by venting to the third member.
- Talk it out and, when we remain split on a technical call, let the owner of the affected module decide. Their call is final for that module and everyone commits to it.
- Take cross-cutting disagreements, meaning anything touching scope, schedule, or the rubric, to a full-team decision rather than to a single owner.
- Separate disagreement from obstruction. Once a decision is made, arguing is over and the work proceeds, even for whoever lost the argument.
- Escalate to Professor Mota only after we have genuinely tried the above, and only for problems affecting the deliverable, such as a member becoming unreachable or work not arriving.

## Deadlines
**We agree to:**

- Set an internal deadline at least 48 hours before every external one, so review and submission are not the same act.
- Track due dates in the vault, where a bot posts them to our Discord announcements channel and pings the team when something is due within a day. Nobody's deadline awareness should depend on remembering to check Canvas.
- Flag a slipping task as soon as we know it will slip, not on the due date. Early warning gives the team options; a missed deadline gives it none.
- Have the whole team review a graded deliverable before it is submitted, since one member submits on behalf of all three.
- Treat unfinished work as the team's problem once it is raised. Whoever has capacity picks up the remainder rather than letting it fail on principle.
